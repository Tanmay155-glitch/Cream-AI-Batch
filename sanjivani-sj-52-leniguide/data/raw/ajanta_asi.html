About Ajanta Caves

The Ajanta Caves are 30 rock-cut Buddhist cave monuments dating from the 2nd century BCE to about 480 CE in Aurangabad district of Maharashtra, India. The caves include paintings and rock-cut sculptures described as among the finest surviving examples of ancient Indian art.

Notable Caves

  • Cave 1: Famous for Padmapani and Vajrapani bodhisattva murals
  • Cave 2: Well-preserved paintings of palace scenes and royal processions
  • Cave 9 & 10: Earliest chaitya halls (1st-2nd century BCE)
  • Cave 16: Contains the famous "Dying Princess" mural
  • Cave 17: Exceptional preservation of Jataka tale paintings
  • Cave 19: Elaborate chaitya hall with intricate facade
  • Cave 26: Features the majestic 7-meter reclining Buddha in Mahaparinirvana

UNESCO World Heritage Site

Inscribed as a World Heritage Site in 1983, the Ajanta Caves represent a masterpiece of Buddhist religious art that influenced Indian art development for centuries.
