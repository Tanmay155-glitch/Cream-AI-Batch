About Ellora Caves

Ellora is an archaeological site featuring 34 rock-cut cave temples representing Buddhist, Hindu, and Jain monuments created between the 6th and 10th centuries CE. The site demonstrates the religious harmony prevalent during ancient Indian civilization.

Visitor Information

  • Opening Hours: Sunrise to Sunset (approximately 6:00 AM to 6:00 PM)
  • Weekly Holiday: CLOSED on Tuesdays
  • Entry Fee (Indian Citizens): Rs 40 (cash) / Rs 35 (online)
  • Children under 15 years: FREE entry
  • Best Time to Visit: October to March (pleasant weather)

Cave Groups

Buddhist Caves (1-12)

  • Cave 5: Largest Buddhist vihara with dining hall features
  • Cave 10: Vishwakarma (Carpenter's Cave) - chaitya hall with Buddha in teaching posture
  • Cave 12: Three-storied 'Tin Thal' vihara with elaborate carvings

Hindu Caves (13-29)

  • Cave 14: Transitional cave showing Buddhist and Hindu elements
  • Cave 15: Dashavatara Cave (Ten Incarnations of Vishnu)
  • Cave 16: Kailasa Temple - monolithic masterpiece carved from single rock, dedicated to Lord Shiva
  • Cave 21: Rameshvara Cave with exquisite river goddess sculptures
  • Cave 29: Dhumar Lena with impressive Ravana shaking Mount Kailasa panel

Jain Caves (30-34)

  • Cave 30: Chhota Kailasa (Little Kailasa) modeled after Cave 16
  • Cave 32: Indra Sabha - finest Jain cave with ornate carvings and lotus ceiling
  • Cave 33: Jagannatha Sabha with seated Tirthankara figures
  • Cave 34: Final cave representing culmination of Ellora's rock-cut tradition

UNESCO World Heritage Site

Inscribed in 1983, Ellora represents the epitome of Indian rock-cut architecture spanning three major religions over 500 years.
